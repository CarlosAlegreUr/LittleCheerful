# Evidence-Based Learning Techniques

These techniques are scientifically proven to enhance learning and retention.

## 1. Feynman Technique
**What:** Explain concepts in simple terms as if teaching a complete beginner.

**Why:** Reveals gaps in your understanding. If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough.

**How:** After learning something, try to teach it back to me or write an explanation without looking at sources.

---

## 2. Active Recall
**What:** Retrieve information from memory without looking at notes.

**Why:** Strengthens memory pathways more effectively than re-reading. Forces your brain to work harder.

**How:** After studying, close all materials and try to remember key points. Quiz yourself regularly.

---

## 3. Spaced Repetition
**What:** Review material at increasing intervals over time.

**Why:** Fights the forgetting curve, builds long-term retention. Cramming doesn't work; spacing does.

**How:** Review concepts after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month.

---

## 4. Interleaving
**What:** Mix different topics/concepts within a single study session.

**Why:** Improves ability to distinguish between concepts and enhances problem-solving. Builds flexible knowledge.

**How:** Don't study one concept for hours; switch between related concepts every 20-30 minutes.

---

## 5. Elaborative Interrogation
**What:** Constantly ask yourself "why" and "how" questions about the material.

**Why:** Builds deeper understanding and connections between concepts. Moves beyond surface memorization.

**How:** For every fact you learn, ask "Why is this true?" and "How does this work?" and "How does this connect to what I already know?"

---

## 6. Concrete Examples
**What:** Create specific, real-world examples of abstract concepts.

**Why:** Makes abstract ideas tangible and memorable. Your brain remembers stories and examples better than definitions.

**How:** For each concept, generate 2-3 examples from your own experience or real life.

---

## 7. Drawing Diagrams by Hand
**What:** Sketch concepts, relationships, and processes on paper.

**Why:** Engages visual and motor memory, builds spatial understanding. The act of drawing forces deeper processing.

**How:** After learning a concept, draw a diagram or concept map from memory without looking at sources.

---

## 8. Practice Testing
**What:** Test yourself on material, even before you feel ready.

**Why:** Retrieval practice strengthens memory and identifies weak spots. Testing is learning, not just evaluation.

**How:** Use my questions, or create your own questions and answer them.

---

**Remember:** Learning is like physical training. These techniques are your exercises. Use them consistently for best results.
